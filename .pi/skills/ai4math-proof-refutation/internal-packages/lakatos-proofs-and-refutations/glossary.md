# Glossary

**Concept stretching / 概念拉伸** — Broadening a concept so previously excluded objects become admissible tests; productive when it exposes theoretical limits, destructive when semantic change becomes arbitrary. (Ch 1)

**Counterexample, global / 全局反例** — A case that falsifies the main conjecture. (Ch 1)

**Counterexample, heuristic / 探试的反例** — A case that may leave a restricted theorem logically intact while exposing poor explanatory reach, narrow scope, or a promising direction for deeper theory. (Ch 1)

**Counterexample, local / 局部反例** — A case that falsifies a lemma or proof step, whether or not it falsifies the main conjecture. (Ch 1)

**Deductive guessing / 演绎的猜测** — Generating a conjecture from a proof construction or problem structure rather than relying only on inductive pattern extrapolation. (Ch 1)

**Dominant theory / 主导理论** — A theory treated as sufficiently secure to serve as the target language/background for translating another theory. (Ch 2)

**Exception-barring / 例外排除法** — Restricting a conjecture's domain to exclude counterexamples, with risk of ad hoc or over-conservative conditions. (Ch 1; App 1)

**Falsity-transfer principle / 虚假性转送原理** — Regulatory demand that a global counterexample should also falsify a relevant lemma; failure indicates incomplete proof analysis. (Ch 1)

**Formal proof / 形式证明** — A derivation whose validity, relative to fixed formative/logical terms and rules, does not depend on the meanings of the specialized descriptive terms. (Ch 2)

**Hidden lemma / 隐藏引理** — An unstated assumption required by a proof; often exposed when a global counterexample leaves all explicit lemmas untouched. (Ch 1; App 1)

**Lemma incorporation / 引理并入法** — Turning a falsified proof dependency into an explicit condition of the improved conjecture. (Ch 1)

**Many-proofs/many-refutations / 多证多驳法** — Developmental method using multiple proof ideas and counterexamples to expand content and expose alternative theorem scopes. (Ch 1)

**Monster-adjusting / 怪物校正法** — Reinterpreting a counterexample so it conforms to the theorem; acceptable only with independent structural justification. (Ch 1)

**Monster-barring / 怪物排除法** — Definitional exclusion of a counterexample from the object class; risky when it protects a theorem without proof-based motivation. (Ch 1)

**One-proof/many-refutations / 一证多驳法** — Rules 1–3: decompose one proof, search global/local counterexamples, incorporate exposed lemmas, and reveal hidden assumptions. (Ch 1)

**Proof analysis / 证明分析** — Decomposition of an informal proof into non-trivial lemmas that can be criticized independently. (Ch 1)

**Proof-generated concept / 证明引生概念** — A concept or definition extracted from a condition that a proof operation needs; its rationale is tied to a proof-ancestor. (Ch 1; Apps 1–2)

**Translation procedure / 翻译程序** — Mapping an informal or less secure theory into a dominant theory; the mapping is fallible when it claims to preserve meaning. (Ch 2)

**Uniform convergence / 一致收敛** — In the appendix's case study, the hidden uniformity condition needed to repair a proof about continuity of a limit; a canonical proof-generated concept. (App 1–2)
